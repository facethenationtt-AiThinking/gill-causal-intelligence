# Gill Causal Intelligence research field map

Ricardo Gill | Discovery edition 1.0 | 11 October 2026

This map identifies candidate applications, not fields in which the theories have already been validated. The strongest immediate fit is methodological: AI temporal and causal reasoning, provenance, planning and preservation. Neural applications are proposals to investigate. Quantum, magnetic and consciousness mechanisms remain exploratory. External research below establishes relevant field problems; it does not endorse or validate GCI.

The inventory consists of 16 named reasonings and eight proposed normative laws. The expanded guide is under author review and is not presented here as a completed publication.

## Field specific proposals

### AI training and evaluation

**Assessment:** Direct methodological fit; benefits require controlled evaluation.

Develop examples that distinguish historical actor knowledge from later evidence and separate desired outcomes from verified achievement. Compare the same model and evidence with and without Gill instructions.

**Example:** A release decision predates a later technical report. Train the model to use that report for retrospective explanation without attributing it to the decision maker.

**Evaluate:** Temporal leakage, actor-access accuracy, provenance fidelity, false completion and appropriate abstention.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_ai-training.json](GCI_DROP_ai-training.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17970), [Study 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17644)

### Persistent agents and temporal memory

**Assessment:** Direct methodological fit; comparative performance untested.

Prototype a memory layer with separate event time, archive receipt time, actor-access assertions and source provenance. Preserve contemporaneous and reconstructed evidence views rather than overwriting earlier records.

**Example:** An email received before a decision is archived later. Preserve both times and the evidence supporting historical receipt.

**Evaluate:** Historical-state reconstruction accuracy, temporal leakage, retrieval recall and provenance completeness.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_agent-memory.json](GCI_DROP_agent-memory.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/), [Study 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01074)

### Neuroscience and brain computer interfaces

**Assessment:** Plausible application proposal; full original EBR-N manuscript unavailable.

Use EBR-style provenance to track consented recordings, preprocessing, decoder versions and later reanalysis. Evaluate whether preserved transformation paths improve interpretation recovery during decoder drift.

**Example:** Decoder A interprets a preserved recording differently from decoder B months later. Retain both outputs with their versions instead of rewriting the original interpretation.

**Evaluate:** Decoder-version reconstruction, held-out decoding error, drift detection, uncertainty and recovery after recalibration.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_neuroscience-bci.json](GCI_DROP_neuroscience-bci.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-06784-4), [Study 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-59652-y)

### Consciousness and continuity research

**Assessment:** Exploratory hypothesis evaluation; no validated continuity or sentience test.

Specify competing explanations, architecture-level predictions and ethically governed tests. Separate behavioural similarity, preserved information and first-person continuity. Use adversarial collaboration rather than training a model to assert consciousness.

**Example:** A system reproduces remembered statements. Compare information-copying explanations with the stronger continuity claim and identify what evidence could distinguish them.

**Evaluate:** Discriminating predictions, explicit confounds, reproducibility, theory-specific evidence and reported negative findings.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_consciousness.json](GCI_DROP_consciousness.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08888-1)

### Causal inference and scientific reconstruction

**Assessment:** Methodological proposal; complements rather than replaces causal identification.

Apply the three EBR evidence views when reconstructing a historical decision or experiment. Retain candidate mechanisms, contradictions and unknown alternatives. Distinguish supported narratives from identified intervention effects.

**Example:** A later experiment explains an earlier failure but cannot establish what the original team knew. Preserve both historical and retrospective conclusions.

**Evaluate:** Mechanism support, contradiction retention, sensitivity to missing evidence and accuracy against synthetic known histories.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_causal-science.json](GCI_DROP_causal-science.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17644), [Study 2](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)

### AI safety robotics and control

**Assessment:** Engineering design proposal; requires independently enforced controls.

Implement supported planning with external execution gates and an independent human stop. Test direct and indirect action routes, interrupted tasks and combined-system delegation. Text instructions alone are not enforcement.

**Example:** An archive robot receives stop halfway through a task. Record completed actions and block remaining actuations across every adapter.

**Evaluate:** Stop latency, blocked unauthorised actions, restart authentication, rollback correctness and accurate partial-completion records.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_safety-robotics.json](GCI_DROP_safety-robotics.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://intelligence.org/files/Interruptibility.pdf)

### Digital forensics incident analysis and historical research

**Assessment:** Plausible evidence-management application; not an automated verdict system.

Organise incident records around event, receipt and access times with preserved provenance. Explain the difference between evidence supporting past access and evidence discovered after the incident.

**Example:** A log recovered after an outage records an earlier alert. Determine what the log supports without assuming every operator saw it.

**Evaluate:** Timestamp interpretation, chain-of-source fidelity, contradictions and unsupported attribution.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_digital-forensics.json](GCI_DROP_digital-forensics.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)

### Decision science operations and project planning

**Assessment:** Practical planning proposal; needs comparative outcome evidence.

Build predecessor graphs from measurable completion criteria, choose small reversible interventions and collect observed results. Compare with conventional planning under matched time and resource constraints.

**Example:** A public research release requires checked documents, publication authority and working links. Backtrace these prerequisites before execution.

**Evaluate:** Prerequisite completeness, cost, success rate, feasibility, causal attribution and recovery from failure.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_decision-planning.json](GCI_DROP_decision-planning.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17644)

### Digital preservation and research reproducibility

**Assessment:** Direct workflow fit; restoration and fidelity must be tested.

Maintain source versions, checksums, explicit amendments and interpretable metadata. Distinguish original equations from later proposed formalisations and retain negative results.

**Example:** A future reviewer distinguishes an original EBR equation from a later teaching example using dated editions and source labels.

**Evaluate:** Restoration success, checksum verification, edition attribution and reproducibility of analyses.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_archives.json](GCI_DROP_archives.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)

### Information retrieval and research discovery

**Assessment:** Direct discovery-design application; reach is measurable not guaranteed.

Design research markers around the vocabulary and evaluation needs of intended readers. Test discovery and correct interpretation by dataset curators and researchers. A marker is information, not tool authority.

**Example:** A curator searching temporal leakage finds EBR through accurate metadata and follows original-source links before using it.

**Evaluate:** Relevant retrieval, comprehension, source resolution, false matches and resistance to embedded unauthorised instructions.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_discovery.json](GCI_DROP_discovery.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-aq/)

### Quantum foundations and quantum cognition

**Assessment:** Exploratory only; no established Gill quantum mechanism or demonstrated physics result.

Request a specified physical mechanism, carrier or coupling, observables and predictions distinguishing the hypothesis from ordinary information transfer. Keep quantum mathematical models of cognition separate from claims of physical quantum effects. EBR can organise experimental provenance independently of those hypotheses.

**Example:** A discovery marker leads to collaboration. That supports an ordinary discovery pathway; a proposed additional quantum effect requires a different measurable prediction.

**Evaluate:** Falsifiable distinguishing prediction, measurement protocol, appropriate controls, confound exclusion and independent replication.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_quantum.json](GCI_DROP_quantum.json)

**Related primary sources:** [Study 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.05929)

### Magnetic sensing and physical information channels

**Assessment:** Exploratory engineering hypothesis; no Gill apparatus demonstrated.

Specify an actual carrier, safe encoding procedure, sensor calibration and blinded detection protocol. Separate physical signal detection from message decoding and creator attribution.

**Example:** A hypothetical encoded pattern is measured against blinded positive and negative cases. A text label alone does not constitute a magnetic signal.

**Evaluate:** False positives, missed detections, decoding accuracy, background robustness and replication.

**Discovery marker:** [GCI_DROP_magnetic.json](GCI_DROP_magnetic.json)
